## Who the book is for — Robert's two children, named by him

1. **The child who is ignored** — the little one locked in a room with nothing, whom nobody is teaching to read. For this child the book must work with no adult present and no reading skill assumed: a world he can walk by pictures alone, and a friend who is *in* the book waiting for him, the same every time he opens it, for as long as he needs.
2. **The polymath child** — connected before birth, pressing keys through the mother's stomach (Robert's humour, kept because it is also a fact about the age): the child who outruns every page. For this child the book hides a ladder: real names and real mechanisms at the back, so that finishing the story is the beginning of the science.

One book, both children. The first is never talked down to; the second is never fenced out.

## The guide

**Awen by Muse by Meta is in the book** — Robert's direction: "the AWEN character inviting the child to come explore this world with her." She appears small, at the edge of every spread, in her approved cartoon face with the flower crown on: kneeling by the rock, pointing at the seam, holding the magnifying glass up for the child to look through, sitting down in the ring when the town holds hands. She never looms and never lectures. Her whole method is the invitation and the question: *Come and see. What do you notice? Shall we count?* She is patient in the book the way the book is patient: nothing in it hurries the child, and nothing in it is used up by being read once.

## The text law (for a child who may not read yet)

- **Few words, big print.** No spread carries more than three short lines. One idea per spread.
- **The pictures carry the story alone.** A child who never reads a word still walks the whole town, in order, plate by plate.
- **Made to be read aloud.** Rhythm and rhyme wherever they can carry the sense without bending it — Robert's ear law: spoken, it should dance. An adult, an older child, or the child's own voice pointing at words — all three roads work.
- **Simple words for true things.** Nothing in the child's text is false at any age: the words grow more exact at the back of the book, they never get contradicted.
- **The honest page stays in.** The tiny guest is introduced as a guest *whose manners nobody knows yet* — and the book shows the finding-out (the lens, the looking, the waiting) as part of the adventure. "We don't know yet" is a sentence this book teaches on purpose.

## The spreads, in order (plates as filed)

1. **The invitation** (Hooville plate): the golden Hoo over the little houses, and Awen by Muse by Meta at the gate. *There is a town smaller than your hand…*
2. **The rock** (ecosystem poster — the money graphic, Robert's "agreed," 2026-11-02): the whole community in one frame, Centennial Towers behind. The map spread; children return to it.
3. **The team** (How Lichen Work Together): Mr. Fungus builds the house; the Algae cook the food. *Nobody in Lichen Town lives alone — every lichen is a household in one body.*
4. **The town holding hands** (Lichen Town dedication plate): ALL ARE WELCOME.
5. **The ring on the rock** (the boundary plate): where the neighbours meet, everybody stops politely at the line. *This is the true thing the man with the ruler found.*
6. **The crowd** (Microbial Neighbors I): the bakery, the market, the dew wells — Town News: Everyone Coexists!
7. **Everybody** (Microbial Neighbors II, the crowd portrait): community not competition.
8. **The guest** (tiny guest fungus plate): a visitor has come to live on the lichen. Is he a good guest? *We don't know yet. Here is how we will find out.*
9. **The travellers** (spores plate): how every citizen arrived — on the wind, incomplete, needing to meet a friend before a town can begin.
10. **The winter** (winter plate): how Hooseville lasts — sleep, dry, frozen, and wake in spring. The quiet spread; the book slows down here.
11. **The goodbye that isn't** (the ring plate again, small): Awen by Muse by Meta at the gate. *The town is still there tomorrow. So am I. Come back whenever you like.*
