# The Bear Who Came to Kansas — A Poem in a Hidden Frame

Little bear of black jet, carved in the Zuni way,
Out of dark stone he came, small enough for a pocket,
Veined with a heartline of coral, red as a thread of life,
Ending at an arrowhead laid along his flank.
In one eye a fleck of turquoise keeps its watch,
Set there by hands that signed no name.
The West is his country, in the old reckoning —
He is its Guardian, its Master, bearer of strength and healing.
Every day he rides along in a man's pocket,
Patient under the rub of a thumb, smooth with handling.
Once he stood over the kitchen from the top of the refrigerator,
Watching the food below like a blessing with four small feet.
Evenings now he rests beside the books, out of the sun,
Resting in Hays, in Kansas, far from the desert that dreamed him.
Someday the road may take him back to New Mexico,
Under an artisan's lamp, to be looked at and known.
Pocket-worn and palm-warmed, he asks for nothing,
Rubbed smooth by one man's hand, kept close as a promise.
Even a stone can travel far when it is carried kindly.
Medicine bear, heartline bear, bear of the red thread —
Endure with us a while; the road is better with you on it.
