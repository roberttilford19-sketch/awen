A story in five movements. The vowels die one at a time — first U, then O, then I, then A — until only E is left, and the bear rests in the one sound that remains.

## 1

A hand carved the little bear from black jet, in the Zuni way, and set a heartline of red coral running from his mouth along his flank to an arrowhead. One eye is turquoise, bright as a bead of summer sky. In the six directions of his people, the bear stands at the West: Guardian and Master of that quarter, a bearer of strength and of healing. He is small, seven centimetres from nose to tail, and unsigned, as such carvings are — the maker trusted the stone to speak.

## 2

Then came the long ride east. Packed in soft cloth, in a box, the bear crossed plains and rivers, past fields and small towns, sleeping in the dark as the miles fell behind him. At last the ride ended in Hays, in Kansas; the box opened in a small apartment, and Robert lifted him into lamplight. The traveling was done. The bear had his man, and his man had him.

## 3

Days settled in a rhythm. The man carried him daily, in a denim nest at his hip, and in idle times his palm pressed the little bear, again and again, till the jet gleamed. At mealtimes the bear sat high in the kitchen, at the fridge's white rim, a silent blessing, watching the bread and the milk and the cheese.

## 4

Then the man gave the bear a new place. The bear rested deep between the tales, where the day's harsh glare never reached. A shelf near the lamp became the bear's haven: safe, shaded, calm. At evenfall the lamp gleamed, and the red thread that ran the flank's length held the warmth. The man's hand went back day after day, and rested near the bear, and the bear kept the same blessed watch, as ever.

## 5

He rests here, where the shelves keep the texts. The jet emblem sleeps, serene, blessed, the West's keeper. Strength rests here yet. He never left; even the deep rest keeps the emblem, ever.
