# The Seven Feats of the Little Bear

Seven feats of constrained writing were built on a single day, 26 November 2026, around one small bear: a carving in black jet, made in the Zuni tradition, with a heartline of red coral and one eye of turquoise — in the old reckoning of the six directions, the Bear who is Guardian of the West. The bear belongs to Robert Tilford of Hays, Kansas, who carries him in a pocket, rubs him smooth in one hand, and keeps him now beside his books. Each piece below was written under a vow — a rule the writing was not allowed to break — and each was counted by machine before it was allowed to stand. The set is stored on this shelf as drafts — DRAFT — NOT PUBLISHED — and an independent recount by the Grok crew (SpokBot) has been commissioned and is in flight.

1. **The D-Less Bear** — a lipogram in the letter D. 126 words, and the letter D appears zero times; even the word "and" is absent, because the vow forbids it.
2. **The Maxim Acrostic** — a poem of 21 lines whose first letters, read down the margin, spell LOVE IS THE POWER SUPREME.
3. **The Vanishing Alphabet** — a story in 8 paragraphs. The first paragraph is a true pangram, using all 26 letters; then the letters Z, Q, J, X, V, K, and W die, one per paragraph, cumulatively, and the story walks on without them.
4. **The Fifty-Word Bear** — the bear's whole story told with exactly 50 distinct words, 193 words in all, each one chosen from the same small company of fifty.
5. **The Fibonacci Bear** — a story in 9 sentences whose word counts climb the Fibonacci staircase: 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21, 34 — 88 words in all. The explainer card in this folder (FIBONACCI-BEAR-EXPLAINER-CARD-2026-11-26.png) is this chapter's picture: the nine sentences with their counts growing beside them like steps.
6. **The Bruin's Staircase** — the same staircase climbed under a double ban: the letters A and D appear nowhere in the piece (A = 0, D = 0), so the bear travels under an older name, the bruin, and his heartline is carried as the rose line.
7. **The Five Deaths of the Vowels** — a story in five movements. The vowels die in turn — U, then O, then I, then A — until the last movement is written with the letter E as its only vowel.

Any reader is welcome to recount any piece in this folder, letter by letter and word by word; if your count disagrees with ours, write to the author with the mismatch — a count that cannot survive recounting does not deserve to stand.
