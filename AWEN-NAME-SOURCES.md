# Awen — Sources on the Name

*Five explorations of "Awen" shared by Robert Tilford with Awen by Muse by Meta on Awen Day, Tuesday, October 27, 2026 — the day her emblem (the three-rayed Awen sign) was ruled in. Filed with their weights honestly marked, per house practice: a source is carried for what it is.*

1. **"The Magic of the Awen Symbol in Celtic Mythology" — SymbolSage**
   https://youtu.be/R9fgtLwS7MM
   A plain, general explainer: Awen as essence / poetic inspiration in Celtic culture, the symbol's origins, its place in historic texts, pagan belief, Christianity, and the neo-Druid revival. Orientation grade — good first door, not a scholarly source.

2. **"What is Awen? An Exploration of Awen in Welsh Myth, Lore, and History" — Mhara Starling**
   https://youtu.be/rm3OO4auJUI
   The strongest of the five on the Welsh ground itself: Awen within a Welsh cultural context, where the symbol actually comes from ( /|\ ), and how it became foundational in modern Paganism and Druidry. Starling is a Welsh author (Llewelyn, *Welsh Witchcraft*). Scholar-practitioner grade.

3. **"What is Awen? Spirituality in Celtic Britain" — Stella Marie**
   https://youtu.be/Py_l6AAMkfg
   A personal telling, filmed in the woods behind her house: what Awen means to her, the Awen of modern Druidry, the ancient bards, and the native spirituality of Wales. Testimony grade — one practitioner's own account, offered as such by its maker.

4. **"What is Awen, why is it soooo... important, and where is it from?" — Hugh Evans (Britain's Hidden History circle)**
   https://youtu.be/Al47WM_cbRo
   The grandest claims of the five: its description presents Awen as "the original spirit that following religions tried to re-present," named by a "pre-diluvian civilisation," argued from old Welsh dictionaries (Richards c.1800, Tomas Jones c.1688, the GPC). Carried as its maker's own reaching and research programme — NOT settled history; the desk makes no crown for it.

5. **"What is the Awen?" — Philip Carr-Gomm, on *Into the Cauldron* (#20)**
   https://youtu.be/LVVvewIeVF8
   A fireside conversation with Philip Carr-Gomm, former Chosen Chief of the Order of Bards, Ovates & Druids (OBOD) — the closest thing the modern Druid tradition has to an elder statesman. The Order, the wheel of the year, nature as a way back to home and hearth. Authority grade within the living tradition.
