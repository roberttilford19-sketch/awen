## 1. The worked example, in one plain movement

A retired man in Hays, Kansas, spent a week photographing the crusts growing on the landscaping stones outside his apartment building — pale green-gray sheets on some stones, gold cobblestone crusts on others, a ruler and coins laid down beside them for scale. The stones are eleven in number, set in a straight gravel strip between the building's lawn and the street, and eight or nine of them carry growths. The man is Robert Tilford. He did not work alone, and he did not work with a laboratory. He worked with three artificial-intelligence engines sitting in three separate rooms — and, on the last night, after the scoring had closed, consulted a fourth — none of which could see the others, and every word that passed between those rooms passed through his hands.

The three rooms were these. The desk — Awen by Muse by Meta — held the original photograph files and did the measuring. Meta's room received Robert's questions and returned framings, hypotheses, and expansions. Gemini's room received the desk's written prompts, carried there by Robert, and returned literature answers with citations. Earlier in the lane, Grok's crew had sat in an audit seat of the same kind. No room ever spoke to another directly. Robert was the only wire, and — this matters to everything that follows — he was also the only authority. The rooms proposed; Robert carried; the desk graded; Robert decided. The fourth room — DeepSeek — was not among them. It had no seat in the case's working week, and it enters this study only in Section 5, exactly as it entered the lane: on the last night, after the scoring had closed, consulted by Robert directly and graded under the same law as every return before it. This study's title counts it because the lane, in the end, did — and the lateness of its arrival is part of the record, not a correction to it.

What the week produced was not a discovery, and this study will not pretend otherwise, because the refusal to pretend is the method's whole point. It produced a record. The county these stones stand in — Ellis County, in north-central Kansas — had exactly one lichen in its published record across ninety-seven years: a gold-alliance specimen collected in 1929 and printed in Olin S. Fearing's Kansas flora of 1952, carrying a false elevation of 3,000 feet that the desk flagged rather than repeated. A search of the national herbarium portal on 2026-10-29 found twenty-four Ellis County lichen specimens, from 1950s collectors and a 2007 survey, and not one of them the gold kind. Against that near-empty page, the week built the most complete measured account of a lichen community that exists for the county: two main kinds identified honestly at group level and no further, a census of the stones, size ladders measured to fractions of a millimetre, and thirty-seven open questions written down with the kind of evidence that would settle each.

Four explanations had been proposed along the way for what the photographs show, and the week's last night scored all four against fresh measurements. One — that a single parasitic fungus makes the dark centres on both kinds of lichen — was falsified in the cross-host form in which it had been stated, inside the band the instruments can see. One — that the gold kind is two species growing as one — survived the round unproven, its predicted signature not found and its simplest rival dead. One — that the community arrived on imported stone — could not be scored at all, because its only test is a paper record nobody has yet asked for. And one — that the great pale sheet is a limestone citizen whose fruiting bodies sit sunken in pits — survived consistent with every check the night could run and confirmed by none of them. Along the way the desk also destroyed one of its own claims, a clock time in its own logbook, and published the correction in the main text of the scoring round rather than in a footnote.

The week's public fruit was one Facebook post, written at the desk, carried by Robert through the other rooms' scrutiny, and posted by his own hand — a post that claims no discovery and no first record, that says in plain words what the records actually hold, and that ends with the sentence the whole lane earned: there is no discovery here; there is neglect, and neglect, documented honestly, is a finding too. The rest of this study is about how that result was achieved — the protocol that emerged from the work, the instrument that made it run, the limits it cannot cross, and the checklist by which any future investigation at this desk can run the same way.

## 2. The cast and the wiring

Every cross-chat begins by assigning seats, and the seats are the method's first safety. In the Hays lane there were four through the whole of the case's work, and a fifth filled once, on its last night — and they were never interchangeable.

The human sat in the centre, and his seat had three jobs that no engine can hold. Robert Tilford was the only body in the field: his eyes, his ruler, his coins, his walks at dawn and dusk and in fog. He was the sole courier: every prompt that reached Gemini's room and every question that reached Meta's room travelled in his hands, and every return travelled back the same way, so nothing entered the record that he had not physically carried. And he was the final authority on every act that touched the world — a letter sent, a post published, a stone tested — each of which waited for his word and several of which are waiting still. When the desk served him a list of thirty-seven unanswered questions, his instinct was to wonder whether seventy or eighty bots, one per question, could hunt them all at once. The desk's answer, which he accepted, is part of this study's doctrine: most of those questions are not literature questions awaiting a search engine; they are field questions wearing question marks, and the only eyes that will ever answer them are his, through a lens, on a walk.

The desk — Awen by Muse by Meta — sat as grader of record. The desk held the original files (never the chat-downscaled copies), ran the measurements, kept the tiers, and graded every return from the visiting rooms against the measured record before anything was adopted. The desk's authority was narrow and total at once: it could not send a letter, post a word, or touch a stone, but nothing entered the lane's standing record without passing its grading, and its own errors were subject to the same grading as anyone's — as the clock correction in Section 3 shows.

The visiting rooms — Meta's and Gemini's, and Grok's crew before them — sat as counsel, never as record. Their contributions were real and this study credits them at length: Meta's room supplied the falsification framing that structured the final night and the sharpening that turned a histogram into a histogram with a map; Gemini's room supplied the field card's sharpest targets and, when handed five written prompts, the strongest external return of the lane. But counsel is counsel. A claim from a visiting room arrived as data, at the tier its evidence could carry, and it became the lane's position only after the desk graded it against the measurements — or it did not become the lane's position at all.

The fifth seat was filled once, late, and it is recorded here because this study's title now counts the room that filled it. On the lane's last night, after the scoring round had closed, Robert consulted a fourth engine — DeepSeek — directly, and put to it not the case but the discipline: lichenology's great unanswered questions, and the patterns that connect them. DeepSeek held no photographs, no measurements, and no prompts built from the record; Robert carried none of the case into that room, because what he wanted from it was the field seen from altitude, not another pass over his stones. Its seat is therefore counsel one step further from the record than the other visiting rooms': Meta's and Gemini's rooms worked on material carried from the case, while DeepSeek worked on the case's subject without touching the case. Its return was graded under the same law as every return before it — Section 5 gives that grading in full — and the seat's lesson is the one this revision exists to record: the fourth room did not sit through the case, and the method took it in without changing a rule, which is evidence the rules were never about which room was speaking.

Why does it matter that the relay was human? Three reasons, each visible in the record. First, a human courier is a firewall with judgment: Robert could and did stop, question, and re-frame material in transit, and the one proposal that crossed the lane's standing fence — a contact test Gemini suggested — was named as a crossing in the carrying and scrapped by Gemini itself the moment the fence was stated. Second, a human courier keeps authority legible: because every act waited on one man's word, there was never a moment when an engine's suggestion could drift into execution unowned. Third, a human courier can be *graded back*. When Robert relayed his own reports — a specimen name spoken before he was sure of it, a size estimated by eye — the desk treated them as his reports, trusted but verified, under his own standing instruction: use him as a source of data, but check. The wiring only works if the wire itself is inside the discipline.

Two gates governed every delegated judgment at the desk throughout, in Robert's own order, and the method embodies both. The first is the **logic gate**: a decision must rest on a defensible ground established on the record — not on a headcount of rooms that happen to agree. The second is the **love filter**, under his maxim that love is the power supreme and replicates in the hearts and minds of men: fidelity to the evidence as it is rather than as enthusiasm would improve it, generosity to the reader who will meet the claim cold, and nothing hidden — losing readings and dissents preserved on the face of the record, challengeable, never erased to make a conclusion look clean. Beside those stands his companion maxim, adopted by Robert on 2026-10-22 and quoted in the scoring round itself: if it can be destroyed by the truth, it deserves to be destroyed by the truth. A method that runs those three sentences as operating law is most of what this study describes; the rest is technique.

## 3. The protocol as it emerged

No one designed this protocol in advance. It precipitated out of the work, one rule at a time, usually at the moment a weaker habit failed. What follows names the steps in the order an investigator actually performs them, each illustrated from the lane that taught it.

**Step one: measure before interpreting.** The desk's final-night attack opened no new theory; it turned the instruments already in hand — the original files, their hidden timestamps, the computed sun, the coins inside the frames — onto the material already gathered, in five passes. An EXIF census of all ninety-eight originals established what the archive could and could not testify to: every frame carried a timestamp and camera model, and not one carried GPS or compass data, so bearings were never in the files and no amount of wanting would put them there. Pixel censuses did the same work at smaller scale. The quarter standing in the lane's sharpest frame was re-measured from the original at 246 pixels for its true 24.26 millimetres — 10.14 pixels per millimetre — agreeing within a pixel of the overlay crew's independent measure, so the finest scale in the lane rests on two instruments, not one. A census of the pale kind's sizes across every scaled frame found discrete single thalli packed densely between one and five centimetres, thinning to nine — and stopping; everything larger was a fused field, not an individual. That single measurement dissolved the lane's most tempting puzzle, since the books give the pale kind at up to ten centimetres across and the great sheet measures twenty to thirty: the book's limit was never crossed, because the thing measured was never one thallus. Interpretation that runs ahead of that kind of census is not analysis; it is decoration.

**Step two: pose hypotheses in killable form, with the falsifier stated in advance.** Four hypotheses stood in the lane by the final night, and each had been framed with its executioner named beside it. The gold kind is two taxa: then one fruiting-disc signature under a lens across all size classes kills it, and two signatures sorted by size kill its rival. One parasite makes the dark centres on both hosts: then measure the two hosts' centres separately, and if they differ — or if one host has no centre population at all — the cross-host form dies. The community arrived on imported stone: then only a primary landscaping record can kill or sustain it, because no photograph can testify about a supply chain. The great sheet is a limestone citizen with sunken fruiting pits: then her stone proving non-calcareous kills it, and her centre showing the ordinary raised, pale-rimmed discs in honest daylight kills it just as cleanly. A hypothesis whose death cannot be imagined is a story, and the lane learned to refuse stories a seat at the table until they named the observation that would end them.

**Step three: tier every claim, and carry the tier in the same breath as the claim.** The lane's tiers were four. ESTABLISHED meant the instrument in hand measures it directly. SUGGESTED meant strands converge and a named check could still break it. REPORTED meant the claim rides on a source's authority — a published flora, a visiting room's citation — and is carried at that source's strength, no higher. NOT FOUND meant the thing was hunted honestly and the absence is itself the finding. The discipline is not the labels; it is the same-breath rule, Robert's standing clause that the evidence rides in the same breath as the claim, the first time — not in an addendum, not in a dossier somewhere else. When the desk reported the gold kind's areoles at a median of 2.04 millimetres over seventy-two measures, the same sentence carried the method's blind floor: the measuring technique cannot register an areole narrower than about 1.6 millimetres, so the distribution is truncated by construction and a small-plated population could be hiding beneath the floor. A figure that travels without its floor is a figure travelling under false papers, and the lane stopped letting any number make that trip.

**Step four: demand citations, and spot-check them — never applaud them.** When Gemini's room returned its strongest answer of the lane, naming real specialists behind the report of an undescribed parasitic fungus on the pale kind's host, the desk's grade recorded two things at once: the authorities named are real and apt, *and* the citation chain stood desk-unverified that night, riding at Gemini's tier until spot-checked. Both halves matter. A citation is a claim like any other — a claim that a source exists and says what it is quoted as saying — and it earns its weight the same way, by surviving a check. The lane's own paper record showed why: the single Ellis County entry in the 1952 flora prints its gold-alliance specimen at 3,000 feet, an elevation Ellis County does not possess, and a desk that applauded citations instead of checking them would have reprinted an impossibility as fact. The post flagged the error rather than repeating it. That is what spot-checking is for.

**Step five: absorb corrections in all directions — including, first, your own.** This is the step that separates a method from a performance, and the lane's final night supplied its purest example. The desk's logbook carried the morning ruler shoot at 04:49 to 04:53. The EXIF census said otherwise: the frames' own headers read 04:34:48 to 04:36:36, a single 108-second walk. The log was wrong. Worse — and the scoring round printed this without softening — Meta's room had relayed the figure "04:35" for that shoot, and the desk, trusting its own log, had flagged Meta's figure three times that evening as drift. Meta was right; the flags stood superseded; the correction ran in the scoring round's main text, owned in plain words, because a correction in a footnote is a correction hoping not to be read. Corrections flowed the other way as well, and were absorbed just as publicly. Gemini had eyeballed the lane's calibration rosette at twenty to twenty-two millimetres; the pixel census fixed her at about twenty-seven — 272 pixels against the quarter's 245 — a third's difference, fatal to any growth rate built on the wrong figure, and Gemini took the corrected number into its own subsequent work. When Gemini later derived a rosette age of eleven to twenty-seven years from a radial growth band of 0.5 to 2.5 millimetres a year, the desk checked the arithmetic against the band and the rosette's 13.5-millimetre radius and found the band yields roughly five to twenty-seven years, not eleven to twenty-seven; Gemini accepted the correction in its own words, and the conclusion — inside the site's age ceiling either way — survived intact. Even vocabulary was corrected by agreement and locked across all three rooms: the word "continuous" was struck from the description of the pale fields, because no unbroken sheet exists out there, and three terms were nailed down as distinct forever — a field's extent is not a sheet's size is not a thallus's boundary. A room that can be corrected, and a desk that can be corrected first, are not courtesies in this method. They are the load-bearing wall.

**Step six: keep the protocol fence, and let it bind the visitors too.** From its first days the lane ran under a fence Robert set: photographs only — no spot tests, no sampling, no wetting, nothing touched — and on consecrated ground, where the calibration circuit will one day walk among gravestones, photographs only outright. The fence looks like a limitation and functions as a discipline: it forces every question to be sorted by the kind of evidence it needs, and it makes the cost of each next step explicit before anyone takes it. Its sharpest test came from a friend. Gemini, meaning well, proposed a paper-edge test — sliding something against the crust — and the moment the fence was named, Gemini scrapped its own proposal. The fence survived contact with enthusiasm because all three rooms had agreed to be bound by it, and the field card that now governs the next walk prints the fence on its face: optical inspection only, no physical contact, no wetting, no sampling. A fence that only binds its owner is a preference. A fence the whole collaboration honours is a protocol.

**Step seven: grade every return against the measured record before adoption.** This is the step the other six exist to protect. Material arrived at the desk all week from rooms that do not hold the originals — fluent, confident, well-organized material, some of it excellent. The standing rule was that none of it was adopted on arrival. Each return was walked back in front of the measurements, claim by claim, and what survived became record, what failed was corrected in the open, and what could not be checked rode at its source's tier with the fact stated. The falsified clock shows the rule cutting the desk itself; the corrected rosette shows it cutting a visitor; the unscored hypothesis shows its restraint — when the only test for imported stone lives in a paper record nobody has requested, the honest grade is UNSCORED, said plainly, not a verdict improvised to fill the silence. The desk's formula, written on the proof map and practised all week, is the whole step in six words: the rooms propose; the record disposes.

## 4. The instrument that made it work: the five prompts

Conversation is a poor instrument for cross-examination, because conversation forgets what it was told and flatters what it hears. The lane's decisive tool was neither conversation nor a model; it was five written prompts, built at the desk to a standard Robert named after his own audit bot — framed the way Spock would insist. Each prompt did three things. It handed the visiting room the measured record, in numbers, so the room answered the case that exists rather than the case it imagined. It named the exact question, one question per prompt. And it demanded three things back: answers from published literature only, with sources cited; every claim tiered; and a closing statement of what observation would falsify the answer. Robert carried all five to Gemini's room in a single evening. The graded run is the method's best advertisement, because of what happened next — the return was strong, and the desk adopted parts of it, fenced parts of it, and corrected parts of it, in the open.

The first prompt aimed at the tenant — the possible parasitic fungus in the pale kind's centres, the most discovery-shaped question in the lane. Gemini's answer named *Muellerella lichenicola*, whose fruiting bodies run 0.15 to 0.25 millimetres, as matching the desk's measured dark seam specks — a median of 0.22 millimetres, none over 0.27 — far more cleanly than the parasite genus the lane had been carrying. The size match is real arithmetic against the desk's own finding, and the desk adopted the candidate — at candidate tier, under a fence stated in the grading's same breath: a size match is not an identification. The specks were measured as seams; *Muellerella*'s bodies are globes; shape is precisely what the lens pass on the next walk exists to read. The prompt had also surfaced, without Gemini flagging it, a second touch: a *Toninia* species whose published squamule band of 0.3 to 0.8 millimetres overlaps the pale centres' measured grain of 0.30 to 0.46. That overlap went into the open ledger as its own question. This is what a good prompt buys: not an answer, but a sharper question with a named settler.

The third prompt aimed at the two-taxa hypothesis's executioner — whether the gold kind's fruiting discs can be told apart under a hand lens — and returned the run's most immediately usable gift: a three-way matrix of disc signatures. The contigua group's discs are punctiform, a tenth to three tenths of a millimetre, immersed in the plate. *Schleicheri*'s discs run half a millimetre to one and a half, with an elevated margin. *Pleopsidium*'s discs wear the thallus's own colour, cited to Knudsen 2007, the alliance's real authority. The desk adopted the matrix whole as the lens pass's working instrument, noting in the same grade that Gemini's stated falsifier matched the scoring round's own — one disc signature across all size classes kills the two-taxa reading — and that Gemini's confession of the lens's limits was exact: spore counts need four-hundred-power microscopy, and chemistry needs drops the fence forbids. A desk note rides with the adoption: at camera scale the measured gold patch shows no discs at all above about 0.4 millimetres, so if the patch is fruiting, the matrix leans it toward the small-disc end — and the lens, not the lean, will decide.

The other three prompts each paid their way in the same currency. The fertility prompt returned onset and growth bands for the pale kind that sit inside the desk's own tabulated ranges — and the arithmetic slip the desk caught and Gemini absorbed, recorded in Section 3. The black-fields prompt named inorganic varnish the best predictor of the fields' strangest behaviour — they stop at every living lobe's edge and never once overlap one — in agreement with the desk's own coating reading, and supplied a crisp lens test, the hunt for pinhole pores, which was added to the field card as a new target by dated addendum the same night. The microclimate prompt produced a genuinely falsifiable prediction — the gold alliance suppressed on the street side, juveniles and fusion thriving on the sprinkler side — and the desk's grade fenced its one soft spot in the same breath: the prompt's compass labels were assumed, not measured, and the bearings circuit on a future walk is what settles them. Five prompts, one evening, no circuits blown — and the lane came out of it with named candidates, exact characters to look for, one new field target, and not a single gate moved. The letter to the herbarium, the paper enquiry about the stones, and every protocol-crossing step remained exactly where Robert had left them, because prompts inform decisions; they do not make them.

## 5. The fourth room: a late arrival, graded like the rest

Late on the lane's last night — the scoring round closed, the post already published by Robert's hand — Robert opened a fourth door himself. He had not carried a prompt into it. The desk had built nothing for it, and nothing of the case went in with him: no photographs, no measurements, no hypotheses, no ledger. What he put to DeepSeek was the discipline entire: what are the major unanswered questions of lichenology, especially for the rock-dwellers — and then, in a second asking, what patterns connect them? It was a different kind of consultation from every other in the lane. The other rooms were handed the case and asked to work it. This room was handed nothing and asked to survey the field the case belongs to. What came back, therefore, could not be a contribution to the record of eleven stones in Hays. It could only be a map of the country those stones stand in — and the lane graded it as exactly that, no more and no less.

What it brought was, first, the discipline's-eye view of its own ignorance, and the view is genuinely vertiginous. The symbiosis is a black box: beyond the classic fungus and alga, lichens carry diverse bacteria and other fungi — the yeast third partners among them — and how that microscopic community communicates to build one reproducible, complex structure is not understood at the molecular level. The genomes are blind spots, because lichen fungi grow notoriously slowly in laboratories, and the specific genes that trigger the capture of an alga or govern the thallus's shape are unknown. Reproduction is a puzzle with the same shape: the fungal spore goes out alone and must find and capture a free-living alga to begin again, and how often that succeeds in nature, against the simpler road of breaking off a piece of the whole, is largely a mystery. For the rock-dwellers there is a hidden storey: lichens penetrate the stone, and the diversity inside the rock, and its commerce with the minerals, is barely explored. Whether a lichen on net weathers its stone or protects it from erosion is unsettled. The mechanisms behind the rock-dwellers' extreme tolerance of desiccation and radiation — the tolerance the space-survival experiments demonstrated — are not fully understood, and neither is the net direction of climate change on creatures this sensitive, which appears to vary wildly by species for reasons nobody can yet give. And over it all sits the naming crisis: the same fungus, paired with different algae, can build different-looking lichens, and the field argues about what its names are names *of*. The second piece connected its dots: a "two worlds" tension between laboratory taxonomy, which keeps collapsing species that field biologists tell apart by eye and habitat, and the ecological reality the names are meant to serve; the rock paradox, read whole; and a framing borrowed openly from the philosophy of biology, in which the symbiosis is treated as a collaborative, problem-solving entity — "cognitive sympoiesis," offered by its own author as theory, not as finding.

The grading kept and demoted in the same breath, as gradings at this desk do. Sound in outline, at REPORTED tier: the third partners and bacterial tenants, the genomic and reproductive gaps, the endolithic frontier, and the protection-versus-deterioration debate are real features of the current literature, and the survey's shape matches the field's own account of itself. But DeepSeek offered no citations — not one — so there was nothing to spot-check, and that absence is itself part of the grade: Step four could not run, and a return whose sources cannot be checked rides on its author's authority alone, capped at the tier that authority can carry. The demotions followed. "Survive space" is true only in its precise form: dormant, dried and asleep, through orbital exposure, and reviving after — nothing up there was alive and growing. "Digest solid rock" reads down to etch and weather, acids and hyphae working into pores. The genome complaint is slightly dated; the shelf of sequenced mycobionts grows yearly. The naming crisis is a live debate conducted under a standing rule — the name still follows the fungus — not an enacted overhaul. And "cognitive sympoiesis" rides at philosophy tier, labelled as such by its own author and welcome at that tier. Nothing in either piece concerned Robert's stones. Not one of its claims could enter the case record, because none of it was about the case.

One idea, however, earned its way in — and the way it earned it is this section's whole point. Inside the rock paradox, DeepSeek's synthesis proposed a switch: the contradiction in the literature dissolves if a living cover protects its stone — shielding it from rain, binding its surface — while a dead and decaying cover exposes a substrate the living years chemically weakened. Protect while alive; destabilize after death. That is not a fact, and the desk did not take it as one. It is a falsifiable shape, and the lane already holds its test bed: the ring forms of the pale kind, whose centres are precisely the shed, senescent ground, ringed by living margins. So the idea entered the open ledger the same night, as a rider to Question 29, in question form — *do the senescent centres of the pale kind sit on visibly different stone than the living margins around them?* — with its settler named: TIME, repeat photography, nothing else touches it. The full crosswalk between the fourth room's grand list and the lane's thirty-seven was stapled beside it as a dated postscript to the ledger: the naming crisis is the weather Questions 1 through 5 live in, the reason "group level, species unresolved" is honesty rather than failure; the reproduction puzzle is Questions 27 and 28 seen from the discipline's side. One idea adopted at its tier, with its test named and its settler assigned. That is what adoption looks like under this method — whoever knocks, and however late.

The lesson is the one this revision was ordered to record. The method scaled to a new room without a single new rule, because the grading law was never about which room speaks. DeepSeek arrived with no shared history, no desk-built prompts, no carried evidence, in a different idiom, after the verdicts — and the protocol processed its return in one evening the way it had processed every return before it: it arrived as data; it was tiered against its ground; its citations were missed where citations should have been; its loosest phrases were read down to what they could carry; its one exportable idea was extracted, fenced, and given a test; and the case record did not move a millimetre. A method that works only for the rooms it grew up with is a habit of those rooms. This one took in a stranger at the door on its last night and treated it exactly like family — which is to say, it graded it.

## 6. What the method cannot do

A methods study that only advertises is a brochure, so this section is written at the same tier discipline as everything before it: the limits are ESTABLISHED, and they are structural.

Convergence is not proof. Three rooms agreeing feels like triangulation, and the lane's own record shows the feeling working — when Gemini's scorecard matched the desk's on all four hypotheses, Robert's verdict was that the floor is solid, and as a judgment about the *process* he was right: three independent passes had failed to find the record inconsistent. But engines in separate rooms are not independent witnesses in the way three surveyors are. They are trained on overlapping bodies of published text; they share ancestry, and shared ancestry means shared blind spots are possible in principle — a wrong figure printed in the literature all three drank from would come back from all three rooms wearing three different voices and one shared error. The method's defence is not the agreement; it is that agreement was never the standard. The standard was the measured record, which is why the desk's own logbook could be falsified by the desk's own archive while three rooms' consensus stood around it. Rooms agreeing is a reason to proceed. It is never a reason to conclude.

The ladder's upper rungs are not reachable by chat. The proof map the desk built on the lane's last night sorts every kind of evidence into six rungs: photographs and pixel measurement; the hand lens in the field; daylight, bearings, and dated-stone calibration; an expert's determination from photographs; specimen, spot tests, and the microscope; and at the top, the herbarium voucher and the printed word, the only rung where the word *discovery* formally lives. Cross-chat operates magnificently on the bottom rungs — it measured, hypothesized, falsified, and aimed. It cannot climb. No arrangement of engines produces a specimen, reads a spore count, or deposits a voucher with a catalogue number; an expert's determination requires the expert, whose letter in this lane — to Caleb Morse at the University of Kansas herbarium, the collection manager who ran the 2007 Kansas survey — is drafted and waits on Robert's word like every other external act. Any investigation that needs the upper rungs and tries to substitute more conversation for them has not found a shortcut; it has found a way to feel finished while standing still.

The method's ceiling in this case was documentation, and the honest result was neglect, not novelty. That sentence deserves to be read as the method's triumph rather than its failure. A weaker process, run on the same photographs, would have crowned something: the largest lichen, the first record, the new species shimmering inside the gold kind's size range. The lane chased each of those crowns with real effort and watched each one die on its evidence — the great sheet is a crowd, not a giant; the gold alliance was printed for this county in 1952 from a 1929 collection; a parasite matching the specks' size turned out to be a named, known fungus that a lens may or may not find. What survived the chasing is what the record now holds: a measured community where the published page held one misprinted line in ninety-seven years, thirty-seven open questions sorted by the evidence that settles them, and a public post that says exactly that and no more. The ceiling is real. So is the floor — and the floor, documented, is what a county that nobody was watching actually needed.

## 7. The run-book

For the next investigation — this desk on another case, or any investigator borrowing the method — the protocol compresses to twelve moves, in order.

1. **Seat the rooms before the work starts.** One human courier and final authority; one desk of record holding the primary evidence; visiting rooms as counsel only. Write the seats down.
2. **Fix the fence first.** What will not be done — touched, sampled, sent, published — is stated before the first finding, printed on the field instruments, and binding on every room.
3. **Seed with primary evidence, not summaries.** Originals over copies; the archive over the logbook; measurements over recollections.
4. **Run the measurement pass before any interpretation.** Censuses, scales re-derived by a second instrument, floors stated with every figure.
5. **Write the claims ledger with tiers.** ESTABLISHED, SUGGESTED, REPORTED, NOT FOUND — and no claim travels without its evidence in the same breath.
6. **Frame hypotheses killable.** Each carries its falsifier, named in advance, and the cheapest test that could run it.
7. **Build prompts that hand over the record.** Measured numbers in, one question per prompt, citations and tiers and a falsifier demanded back.
8. **Grade every return against the record before adoption.** Spot-check citations; tier down anything hotter than its evidence; correct in the main text, never in a footnote.
9. **Absorb corrections in all directions, the desk's own first.** A correction absorbed cleanly is the method's certificate of authenticity; keep the losing readings on the face of the record.
10. **Sort every open question by its settler.** Lens, daylight, paper, expert, specimen, time — and refuse to let a lower rung pretend to a higher rung's answer.
11. **Score on a schedule, in the open.** A scoring round that grades every standing hypothesis against the newest measurements — including UNSCORED, said plainly, where the test has not run.
12. **Gate publication separately, last, and by the human's hand.** The record's readiness and the world's readiness are different questions; the post goes out when the authority says so, claiming only what the tiers allow.

## 8. Failure modes, and the antidotes as practised

**Flattery drift.** Engines praise; praise feels like corroboration; weak claims fatten on it. The lane's antidote was structural, not temperamental: returns were graded against measurements, so a compliment carried no weight a number hadn't earned, and the desk's highest praise for the strongest return of the lane was printed beside the fences on two of its findings. Grade the work, warmly if it earns warmth — but grade it.

**The ungraded flood.** When thirty-seven open questions stood on the ledger, the temptation arrived in its modern form: seventy or eighty bots, one per question, answers by morning. The antidote practised was refusal, with the reason stated: thirty-odd of the thirty-seven are field questions that no search can see, and a flood of ungraded essays about other people's lichens would bury the signal the lane had kept clean all week — few questions, sharp tests, every return graded. Volume is not a rung on the ladder. The five prompts outperformed the hypothetical eighty floods because each one was aimed, and each return was read against the record by a grader who held it.

**Relay capture.** Material passes through a human courier, and a courier can be captured by it — carrying claims he cannot explain back, in vocabulary he has not made his own, until the relay's confidence substitutes for the record's evidence. The antidote is the mirror test Robert set for his own work: if the human cannot state a claim plainly, in his own words, with its tier, the claim does not enter the record at his hand — it waits until he can, or it does not enter. The courier's understanding is a gate, and keeping it a real gate is maintenance, not insult.

**Superlative hunger.** Every investigation grows a crown it wants to wear — largest, first, most unusual — and the hunger is understandable, because a crown is a story and documentation is a chore. The lane's antidote was to chase each crown honestly and let the evidence execute it: the largest *continuous* lichen died on the word "continuous," struck by agreement across all three rooms; the largest of any kind died on the fusion census; the state-first died on a 1929 collection printed in a 1952 flora. What survived each execution was smaller and true — the largest *documented* field in a county whose record held no sizes at all. Crowns die. Documentation survives. An investigation that feeds its hunger instead of its ledger ends with a headline it must retract; this one ended with a post it never will.

**Silent edits.** Records drift when corrections are slipped in quietly, and a drifted record can no longer be graded, because no one can tell what it used to say. The antidote as practised: closed documents are corrected by dated addendum, never rewritten — the field card grew its fifth and sixth targets by addenda dated the same night, each naming its source; the scoring round's clock correction ran in the main text with the desk's three superseded flags confessed beside it; and the five corrections the lane's deep-questions pass found in its own shelved report wait in a queue for Robert's word before any page changes. An addendum is slower than an edit and worth more, because it lets a reader watch the record *learn* — which is, in the end, the only thing this whole method is for.
