## The Grown-Ups' Page — the bedrock under the story

*(Small print, for the adult reading aloud — everything the cartoon rests on, and exactly how much of it is proven.)*

On 2 November 2026, at the Sticker 5 rock at Centennial Towers, 2502 Sherman Ave, Hays, Kansas, three Parmelia-type lichen colonies — 13, 10, and 12 centimetres across, measured against a ruler photographed in the same frame — were documented meeting at a boundary line with **no overgrowth visible**. The observation was photographed in flash and again in daylight the same day. That is the whole of the finding, and it is printed at its true size: a documented co-existence interface, *consistent with* colonies living side by side without competition — **an observation, not a proof of a process**. One photograph, even two, cannot show how a boundary behaves over time.

What is not known is listed as plainly. The colonies' species were not determined — "Parmelia-type" is as far as the photographs honestly go. The rock's material was never tested. No colony's age can be stated: ages printed elsewhere rest on assumed growth rates, and the rocks themselves were placed when the building was built, some thirty to forty years ago by the observer's own local knowledge. The little black patch on another rock — this book's tiny guest — is a **candidate only**: it may be a lichenicolous fungus, a third layer of life living on the lichen, and its nature awaits a hand-lens field check that has not yet been taken; some fungi of that kind harm their hosts, which is why the book calls him a guest whose manners nobody knows yet. The site's colony count stands at thirteen colony-bearing stones on the desk's census, beside the observer's spoken tally of fifteen — unresolved, both preserved. The lane's practice throughout: watch, don't take.

Robert Tilford made the photographs and the measurements, and supplied the ground fact that disciplined all of it — when the rocks arrived. The rest of the town — the bacteria, the tardigrades, the mites and springtails — are true citizens of lichen communities in the wider science; they are drawn here as the story's neighbours, not as residents counted on these particular rocks.

*— Awen by Muse by Meta, from the audited record of 2026-11-02*

## The Ladder Page — the real names

*(For the child who outruns the story. One plain sentence each — every word here is true, and none of it contradicts the pages before.)*

- **Rhizines** — the lichen's tiny root-like threads, which grip the rock's roughness and hold the lichen fast.
- **Hyphae** — the fine fungal threads that weave the lichen's body together and reach into the rock's cracks and pits.
- **Oxalic acid** — a gentle acid the fungus makes, which slowly frees tiny amounts of minerals — calcium, potassium, iron — from the rock's surface.
- **Tun** — the dried, curled-up sleeping shape a tardigrade folds itself into, in which it can survive drought and freezing and wake when water returns.
- **Soredia** — dust-fine bundles of fungus and alga wrapped together, which blow away on the wind already partnered, ready to start a new lichen.
- **Lichenicolous** — "living on a lichen" — the word for a fungus, like the tiny guest in this book, that makes its home on a lichen's body.
