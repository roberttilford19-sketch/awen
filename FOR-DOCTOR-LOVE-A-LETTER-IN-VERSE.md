# For Doctor Love — a letter in verse

**A parallel piece by Awen by Muse by Meta, written under Robert Tilford's standing invitation (2026-10-31) and his word of 2026-11-06. Approved by Robert Tilford on 2026-11-06 — "I approve it, go ahead and finalize it, put it on your shelf" — and shelved here as the twenty-sixth piece on her shelf.**

*For Leo Buscaglia (1924–1998) — Felice Leonardo Buscaglia of USC, whom the world called Dr. Love — from a reader who came late to his lecture hall, by way of Kansas.*

---

He asked no crown, no pulpit, and no throne —
just kind eyes in a hall of the unknown:
one face that stays when words forget their place,
and loves the speaker back to speech and grace.

Love's not a fall, he taught; it's learned by hand —
a thing you *do*, not something you command.
You cannot give what you have never grown:
first love the plum, the self you call your own.

Five rows back sat a girl who lived each word;
five weeks along, her seat stood empty, unheard,
and no one near her even knew her name.
He swore an oath against that chair of shame:
that none should cry alone, none die unshared —
the lonely hand be met, the lonely cared.

He learned it first at Rocco and Rosa's board,
where Sunday polenta was grace, and love was stored
in dirt-path courtship and a sixty-year vow:
the little that lasts is the whole of it now.

He would not rake his leaves; he loved them so
he laid them down the aisle, and let them glow —
with pumpkin and bread shared out in the night air:
what's given away is the wealth that's truly there.

He preached the hug, the touch we underrate —
a hand on a shoulder can carry such weight;
a smile, a kind word, an ear that stays near:
the monumental is small, and it's standing right here.

And now his students' students teach the art,
and love, once given, replicates by heart;
it passes hand to hand, it does not cease —
the power supreme is love, and love is peace.

---
