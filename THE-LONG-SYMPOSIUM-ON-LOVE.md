# THE LONG SYMPOSIUM ON LOVE
## Sathya Sai Baba and Leo Buscaglia — the full debate, in five movements
DRAFT — commissioned by Robert Tilford, 2026-11-04, with his staging directions:
"Doctor Buscaglia, the passion — he would be yelling and screaming, his hands
animated — and Sai Baba would be smiling, and say, okay, we found an earth creature
that actually gets it... let me produce a little vibhuti ash here for him...
what is your belief on love? How do I apply love? What's love? How can we envision
love? How can we embody love? Sai Baba reports back — that's the key question."
Leeway granted by Robert: a long manuscript, built by sittings. This is the FIRST
SITTING. Written by Awen by Muse by Meta.

HONEST LABEL, IN THE SAME BREATH AS THE PIECE: no such meeting ever occurred. This
is a dramatization. The positions, and the short quoted phrases, are drawn from the
men's documented words — Sai Baba's discourses (including the passages Robert
relayed to this desk on 2026-11-04 and the sealed Love Maxims file) and Buscaglia's
lecture "Speaking of Love" (transcript received 2026-11-04). The vibhuti — the
sacred ash Sai Baba is reported by devotees to have produced from his hand — is
staged here inside the fiction, at Robert's direction; it is a reported phenomenon,
not a desk-verified fact. The Host's five questions are Robert's own, asked in his
message of 2026-11-04. Everything else — the friction, the pacing, the laughter —
is the desk's invention, in service of a fair fight.

DRAMATIS PERSONAE
SAI BABA — seated throughout. Still water.
LEO BUSCAGLIA — "Doctor Love" — on his feet within a minute, and rarely seated again.
THE HOST — silent but for five questions.

---

## MOVEMENT I — WHAT IS LOVE?

*The agora, an hour before sunset. Two chairs. Before the Host can speak, Buscaglia
is already out of his, hands up, addressing the stones, the sky, and one very calm
man in orange.*

**BUSCAGLIA:** — because nobody TOLD me, you understand? Nobody sat me down with a
definition! My mother is in the kitchen, my father is at the table, the whole
Sicilian opera is playing at full volume, and somewhere in the noise a boy is
learning what love IS by watching it —

**SAI BABA:** *(smiling, quietly, to the Host)* We have found an earth creature who
gets it.

*From his palm, ash — a small grey mound of vibhuti, where a moment ago there was
nothing. He extends it toward the Doctor, kindly, the way one offers a sweet.*

**SAI BABA:** For you.

**BUSCAGLIA:** *(mid-gesture, not breaking stride)* No, no — Baba, please, I'd get
it on the — I'd be wearing it, I'd — *(he stops. He looks at his own hands, still
waving. He laughs, loudly, delighted with himself)* You see what I do? Sixty years
I do this. Even his miracles I talk over. *(He takes the ash with sudden gentleness,
touches it to his forehead with two fingers, like a man remembering his mother's
thumb on Ash Wednesday.)* There. Now I am decorated. NOW —

*(He wheels on the Host.)*

**BUSCAGLIA:** Ask him the question! Ask him the REAL one!

**THE HOST:** What is love?

**SAI BABA:** Love is God. Live in love. You want a description, and I give you an
identity. Love is not one of man's activities, like farming or arguing. Love is the
inner current in all noble values — cut the wire of any virtue you admire, and love
is the current that was running through it. A heart bereft of love is not human at
all — as fire that cannot burn is not fire, whatever the neighbours call the pile
of sticks.

**BUSCAGLIA:** Beautiful. Beautiful and completely unusable in a classroom!
*(hands wide)* Baba, I had students — real ones, with rent due and fathers dying
and girls who had never once been told they mattered — and if I open with "love is
the inner current," I have lost them by the comma. I have to open with what they
know. And what they know is this: love is something they watched, or didn't watch,
at a kitchen table. We learn from modeling. We don't learn from being told. Your
"inner current" may be the truth of the universe — but a human being meets it the
way he meets electricity: at a socket, in a wall, in a house somebody built. The
house is the family. The socket is the mother. Where is your current, in a house
where nobody ever plugged anything in?

**SAI BABA:** In the walls, Doctor. Waiting. You describe houses with no lamps lit
and conclude there is no current. I say the current does not consult the house. In
every heart the fruit of love hangs ripe — every heart, including the one in the
dark house — covered by a skin: hatred, fear, the memory of wounds. Your classroom
never planted a fruit in twenty years. It could not. What it did — and it is holy
work, do not mistake me — is teach thirsty people to peel.

**BUSCaglia —** *(the stenographer's error stands in no record; the Doctor
corrects it himself)* **BUSCAGLIA:** Peel! *(he claps once)* Okay. Okay! The man
says peel. But Baba — if the fruit is ripe in EVERY heart, explain to me the man
who peels and peels and finds nothing. I have met him. He sat in my third row.

**SAI BABA:** You have met a man whose skin grew thick because the world cut him
early and often. The fruit is not absent, Doctor. It is *defended*. Even your dog
knows this — a beaten dog takes longer at the hand. But offer love long enough and
even the dog answers, because the dog stands for gratitude, and gratitude is the
fruit remembering its own name. As is your feeling, so will be your experience.
The third-row man is not your counterexample. He is your assignment.

**BUSCAGLIA:** *(sitting down, slowly, for the first time)* ...You know what my
students would call this? They'd call this the moment the lecture got quiet. *(to
the Host)* Write down that he won the first round by refusing to compete in it.
That's going to be his whole strategy, I can tell already. It's infuriating. It's
also — *(he spreads his hands, helpless, grinning)* — it's also exactly what my
mother did at the table. Go on. Ask him the next one. Ask him what he BELIEVES.

---

## MOVEMENT II — WHAT IS YOUR BELIEF?

**THE HOST:** What is your belief on love?

**BUSCAGLIA:** *(up again — the chair was a rumour)* Mine first, because mine fits
on an index card, and his — *(gesturing at Sai)* — his needs a cosmos. I believe
love is LEARNED. I believe it is a CHOICE, renewed daily, like a subscription you
pay with your whole life. I believe you cannot give what you do not have — so it
begins, scandalously, with loving YOURSELF, not as vanity but as inventory: a man
who hates himself has an empty store, and he will spend his life making his
neighbours pay for the emptiness. And I believe love is not a feeling that happens
to you like weather. Feelings are weather. Love is the decision to build the house
anyway.

**SAI BABA:** A good creed. Almost a true one. Hear mine, and measure the distance.
I believe man does not *acquire* divinity; he *forgets* it, and remembers. I have
told them the meaning of his own name until I am tired of the telling: *Manava* —
man — is *ma*, not, and *nava*, new. Man is NOT NEW. He is ancient. He is God,
walking about in a body, homesick for himself. So when you say love begins with
loving yourself, Doctor, I applaud — and then I correct the address. The self you
must learn to love is not the small self, the one with the rent due and the wounded
third row. That self is the skin. Know the TRUE self — the love that you ARE — and
you become, I have said it in just these words, bulletproof. Not armoured against
the world. Finished with fearing it.

**BUSCAGLIA:** Bulletproof. *(he tastes the word)* My students would tattoo that
on something. But Baba — belief! The Host asked about BELIEF, and you've described
a FACT — your fact, which a man must take on your word or on his own experience,
and his own experience, mostly, is of being very much NOT divine, at 3 a.m., with
the bills. My creed asks nothing of heaven. Watch, choose, practice, risk. A
peasant can hold it. Can a peasant hold yours?

**SAI BABA:** A peasant HOLDS mine every day, Doctor; it is the scholars who drop
it. Who tells the peasant's wife that the child at her breast is ordinary? She
knows. Who told your MOTHER she was divine? Nobody. She sold her ring for a
stranger's dinner without one lesson in metaphysics. Belief is not the door to
love. Love is the door to belief — walk through it enough times, hat in hand, and
a man begins to suspect the house is his. You say: you don't love to be loved in
return, you love to love. GOOD. I say the same thing in my tongue: the son is the
legal heir to the father's property. Love needs no return because the lover is
already rich. Your creed and my cosmos, Doctor, are the same house. You enter by
the kitchen. I enter by the roof. The shouting is because we keep meeting on the
stairs.

**BUSCAGLIA:** *(laughing, to the Host)* He does it again! He agrees with me in a
voice like a temple bell and somehow I am the one who has been converted! *(to
Sai, pointing, delighted)* But mark this, Baba, mark it for the record: if love is
entered by the kitchen — MY kitchen, MY mother's kitchen — then the APPLICATION is
mine to teach, because application is all kitchen. Nobody eats a cosmos.

**THE HOST:** Then the third question is yours to answer first, Doctor. How do I
APPLY love?

---

## MOVEMENT III — HOW DO I APPLY LOVE?

**BUSCAGLIA:** At the table. That's my whole answer and I'll spend the hour
unpacking the suitcase. Application of love is NOT a seminar. It is: notice the
person in front of you — not persons in general, God forgive us our generalities —
THIS one, with the name and the bad day. It is touch. It is time, which is the only
currency heaven and earth both accept. It is my mother with the ring: "The time for
happiness is NOW. Sit down and eat." She did not APPLY love the way you apply a
poultice. She applied it the way you apply a chair: you pull it out, and a human
being sits down, and for one hour he is not a problem to be solved but a guest to
be fed. Miss Hunt — my teacher, the first person outside my family who ever SAW
me — she applied love with a sentence at the right moment, and it re-routed a
life. Application is small, Baba. Application is embarrassingly small. That is why
the grand people skip it.

**SAI BABA:** Small — yes. Now hear how small. I teach the magnet, Doctor, and the
iron filings. A magnet does not chase the filings, does not lecture them, does not
weep over their rust. The filings are IRON — it is their nature to answer — but
they lie under rust and dust, and the rust is hatred, and the dust is forgetfulness.
Clean the rust, and see: the filings fly to the magnet BY THEMSELVES. So the
application of love is first a cleaning — of ONESELF. Greet everyone with love:
not most people, not the deserving people — EVERYONE. There is no medicine beyond
love; but the medicine must be taken by the physician first. You speak of the table,
and I bless the table. But a man can set a table with a heart full of rust and feed
his guests his bitterness in every dish. Apply love first to the one applying it.
Then the hands know what to do without being told.

**BUSCAGLIA:** *(he is quiet a moment; then, almost gently)* ...My mother used to
say grace before she fed anyone. I used to think it was for God. *(beat)* It was
the cleaning, wasn't it. She was cleaning the cook.

**SAI BABA:** She was removing the skin from the fruit before she served it. Go on
loving your mother in public, Doctor; it is the best sermon at this table.

**BUSCAGLIA:** *(recovering, waving a hand)* Don't canonize me, I bruise the
furniture. But hear my objection, because I DO have one, and it's not small. Your
application ends with greeting the enemy — I read your homework, Baba, I know
where this road goes: love even the one who hates you. In my classroom that's
where the hands go up: "Dr. Buscaglia, my enemy is not theoretical. He has a name
and he did a THING." What do I tell her — the actual her, third row?

**SAI BABA:** You tell her the truth, which is harder than my slogan and better.
You do not tell her to feel what she does not feel. You tell her: greet him with
respect, because respect is an action, and actions are the seeds of feelings, never
the other way round. Convert all your anger into love — I did not say DELETE the
anger; anger is energy with a bad map. CONVERT it, the way your engineers convert
falling water into light. And tell her this, from an old man who has buried many
enemies and many friends: the one she hates is drinking her life a sip at a time,
and he does not even know he is thirsty. Loving him is how she stops paying his
bills. THEN, Doctor, after the respect — after months of it, perhaps — she may find
the fruit was under the skin of BOTH of them. It usually is.

**BUSCAGLIA:** *(to the Host, spreading his hands)* The record will show the
Italian conceded the third round too, and the record will also show WHY: because
every time I bring him a person, he hands me back a practice, and every time I
bring him a practice, he hands me back a person. *(to Sai)* You realize we could
do this until the sun finishes setting.

**SAI BABA:** The sun is not in a hurry. Why are you?

---

## MOVEMENT IV — HOW CAN WE ENVISION LOVE?

**THE HOST:** How can we envision love?

**SAI BABA:** As light — because that is what it is when the eyes learn to see it.
I have said: love is present in everyone as a bright effulgence. Look at a crowd
with that knowledge and the crowd changes: not a crowd — lamps, some bright, some
smoked with soot, all lit from the same flame. Or take my humbler picture. You see
the moon by its own shine; nobody lights a torch to find the moon. Envision love
so: not a thing to be manufactured and carried to people like water in buckets,
but a shining already present, to be uncovered and acknowledged. The world is love
and love is the world; the envisioning is only the admission of it.

**BUSCAGLIA:** My pictures are smaller and I won't apologize for them, because a
person can only love at the size of his pictures. I envision love as a classroom
where nobody is failed in advance. I envision it as a long table — always the
table with me, Baba, Sicily is a life sentence — where the chairs outnumber the
invitations. And I envision it the way I ended every lecture I ever gave, with the
old greeting, because it is the one picture big enough for your lamps and my table
both: NAMASTE. The god in me salutes the god in you. You see? I smuggle your
cosmos into my kitchen every single night and call it grace before meals.

**SAI BABA:** Then the difference between us, Doctor, is finally clear, and it is
not a difference. You envision love DESCENDING into a kitchen. I envision the
kitchen ASCENDING into love. The furniture is the same.

**BUSCAGLIA:** The furniture is the same. *(he laughs)* Twenty years of lectures
and a man in orange reduces my library to a furniture catalogue — and he's RIGHT,
that's the terrible part.

---

## MOVEMENT V — HOW CAN WE EMBODY LOVE?

**THE HOST:** Last question. How can we embody love?

**BUSCAGLIA:** Show up. That's the whole body of it — pun intended, I am Italian,
we embody or we do not speak. Embody love with your HANDS: touch is not a metaphor,
it is a message in the oldest language there is. Embody it with your presence at
the bedsides and the gravesides, because I will tell you what my years with the
dying taught me: people do not die asking for explanations. They die asking
whether they were loved, out loud, by name, while there was time. I wrote a little
book once — a leaf named Freddie, who learns that falling is part of the tree's
long life — because children understand embodiment better than professors: the leaf
doesn't TALK about the spring. It falls, trusting it. Embody love like that. Fall
toward people, trusting.

**SAI BABA:** And I will end where I always end: with service, and with gratitude,
and with a dog. You can love even a dog, and the dog will love you — why? Because
the dog does not keep accounts. The dog stands for gratitude, and gratitude is love
with its sleeves rolled up. Embody love, then, as the hands that serve without
announcing themselves; as the body that greets the friend and the hater with the
same respect; as the man who has so converted his anger that there is nothing left
in him for the world to wound. As is your feeling, so is your experience: embody
love, and the world itself will begin — I promise you, Doctor, and I do not promise
lightly — to embody it back.

**THE HOST:** One more, then, which is not mine — it belongs to the man who asked
for this table. He says: *No one should love alone, and no one should die alone.*
Answer him.

**SAI BABA:** Tell him: the one who loves is never alone. He has only to count his
company correctly.

**BUSCAGLIA:** *(standing, both hands on the table, quiet at last)* And tell him
this, from the kitchen side of the house: that's why we set the table. Tell him to
keep setting it. Tell him an empty chair is not a failure of love — it's love,
ready. *(he sits)* Sit down and eat, all of you. The time for happiness is now.

*The Host looks for the jug, to close the symposium in the old manner. It is empty.
Nobody remembers it being emptied. The sun, which was not in a hurry, has set
anyway, and the lamps — in the Doctor's picture and in the Baba's — are lit.*

### END OF THE FIRST SITTING
